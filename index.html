Treatment Workbook

This workbook has three parts: a CBT thought record, a DBT diary card, and the Section 3 assignments with the pages you repeat each week. Fill in what your therapist assigned, then bring it to your next session.

What you type is kept in the browser on this device and is not sent anywhere, including to your therapist. To share it, print it or save it as a PDF and send that. If you clear your browser data, the entries go with it.

Part 1

CBT Thought Record

Use this when your mood shifts and you notice it. Write down the situation while it is fresh, the thought that came first, and how strongly you felt it. Then look at the evidence on both sides and write a thought that fits all of it. Rate the feeling again at the end. One entry a day is plenty.

Columns
  1. Situation: where you were, who was there, what was happening.
  2. Feelings: one word for each feeling, rated 0 to 100.
  3. Automatic thought: what went through your mind. Circle or mark the one that feels hottest.
  4. Evidence for the hot thought: facts, not interpretations.
  5. Evidence against the hot thought: anything that does not fit it.
  6. Balanced thought: a thought that accounts for both columns. Rate how much you believe it, 0 to 100.
  7. Feelings now: rate the same feelings again, and note any new ones.
Part 2

DBT Diary Card

Fill it in each day if you can. Write your three target behaviors from your hierarchy in the first rows, then rate the urge (0 to 5) and mark whether you acted on it. Rate emotions 0 to 5. Rate your overall intensity on your Personal Emotional Thermometer (minus 10 to plus 10). List the skills you are practicing and rate each day with the legend below.

Skill usage ratings
  1. Not thought about or used
  2. Thought about, not used, didn't want to
  3. Thought about, not used, wanted to
  4. Tried but couldn't use them
  5. Tried, could use them, but they didn't help
  6. Tried, could use them, helped
  7. Automatically used them, didn't help
  8. Automatically used them, helped
Part 3

Section 3: Early Treatment Assignments

The first three pages are the early-stage assignments. The pages after them (hierarchy, thermometer, exposure tracking, vulnerability and repair plans) are updated every week alongside the diary card in Part 2.

Change Process Table

List the eating disorder behaviors that get in the way of a life worth living. For each one, work across the row.

Individual Food Blueprint

Our relationship with food is shaped by what we lived through, how we were raised, and our cultural background. Take a few minutes with each question and write what comes to mind.

Safety Exploration Assignment

Safety cues are the signals that tell your body and mind you are safe enough. Notice them over the next two weeks and add to the lists as you go.

DBT Target Behavior Hierarchy

Update this to reflect your current target behaviors. Look back at your admission hierarchy for examples.

Personal Emotional Thermometer

Describe the feelings and body sensations you have at each point from minus 10 to plus 10, and mark each point's color. Green is your usual window of tolerance, where you feel more regulated and grounded. Blue is where you feel shut down or disconnected. Red is where you feel agitated, anxious, or angry. You will use these numbers on the diary card and the exposure tracking pages.

Exposure Tracking: Supporting a Life Worth Living

Rate activation on your Personal Emotional Thermometer, minus 10 to plus 10: before the exposure, at the highest or lowest point, and at the end.

Vulnerability Reduction Plan

Five things you will do this week to lower your vulnerability to the target behaviors (sleep, regular eating, movement within your plan, medication, contact with supports).

Consequence Repair Plan

If a target behavior happens, three things you will do to repair its effects on you, others, or your treatment.
